# Bayline Metro stations: research notes (stations workstream)

Key facts per station that drive the builds (layout, levels, circulation, materials, recognisable cues, ridership).
Sources: Wikipedia station articles; the 1966–68 BART contract drawings (scans in Eric Fischer's Flickr albums);
BART station maps, modernization reports and ridership spreadsheets (average weekday exits, Aug 2026 and FY2019);
511/MTC station maps; Wikimedia Commons photos; OpenStreetMap geometry; satellite imagery. "~" = estimate.
BART Facilities Standards R3.0 (Architecture – Passenger Stations, 2013) for generic dimensions.

## Common

- Platforms 700 ft (213.4 m), 8 ft end transition zones; platform top 0.991 m above top of rail (level boarding),
  edge 1.676 m from the track centreline (confirmed with the trains workstream: car floor 0.991 m, half-width 1.600 m).
- Edge: ~0.6 m yellow truncated-dome strip with dark-grey pads at the door positions; cantilevered edge over an
  under-platform refuge; subway trackways have a depressed undercar trench; third rail (white fibreglass cover) on
  the side away from the platform.
- Escalators 48 in, 30°, 3 flat steps, 100 fpm; public stairs ≥ 5'6", 6¾ in risers / 12 in treads; handrails 2'10";
  ceilings ≥ 10 ft; TVMs 3'4" x 2'10⅝" x 6'6" at 4 ft centres; bins ~70 ft apart.
- Downtown entrance canopies (2018–27): thin flat white roof on slender posts, glass sides, stainless pylon with the
  station name and a live train display, roll-down gate.
- Many 1970s aerial stations roof only the **middle ~85–110 m** of the platform; the open ends carry a central beam
  or T/L-frames with signs, displays and lights.
